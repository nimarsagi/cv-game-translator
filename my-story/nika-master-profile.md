# PART I — THE STORY

*Structure loosely follows Christopher Vogler's 12-stage adaptation (*The Writer's Journey*) of Joseph Campbell's monomyth (*The Hero with a Thousand Faces*).*

**Throughline:** one constant curiosity about what is real beneath the surface, applied through ever-better vehicles.

### Chapter 1 — The Ordinary World
Nika began as an enthusiastic, optimistic, curious kid: the one who drew attention in a room and collected friends easily. He also made a point of getting to know the classmates others left out or dismissed as "weird", and understanding them made him less quick to judge anyone. He started secondary school in VMBO, the pre-vocational track. He carried an early calling: to become a doctor, like generations of his family before him. That goal lifted him into HAVO. But somewhere along the way he realised the dream wasn't his. He was chasing it to make his parents proud, hoping it would make him happy, and it didn't sit right. He kept what was genuinely his, the drive to help people, and let go of the inherited role. He finished VWO simply because he loved learning. One of his scripts was performed as a theatre play at his school, an early sign of what came next.

### Chapter 2 — The Artist's Detour
After VWO, Nika followed a second call: storytelling. He gave himself a year to test it for real. He wrote screenplays, worked on a set as an assistant producer, and directed his own film, which remained unfinished when the budget ran out. The year taught him something harder than any film school could have. He loved stories, but not the daily grind of writing, and not the slow climb through low-budget productions. He had wanted to start at the top without paying the entry price. Once he saw that price clearly, he chose not to pay it. Meanwhile, a quieter pursuit took root again: trading.

### Chapter 3 — Finding the Real Call
Looking back, Nika noticed what had actually held him in film. It wasn't the writing or the sets. It was the people inside the stories: how minds work, how people struggle, what drives them. He spent hours watching interviews with people facing mental challenges, trying to understand them from the inside. Storytelling had been the vehicle; the human psyche was the destination. In 2023 he enrolled in psychology at the University of Amsterdam.

### Chapter 4 — The Mentors and the Gift
At university, Nika found his mentors among the methods professors, the ones who questioned the rules of psychological science instead of just teaching them. From them he took his most important tool: the habit of digging beneath any conclusion to the assumptions and methods holding it up. He tested that tool in the markets. Statistics and machine learning first drew him toward quantitative trading, where he gained experience modelling data. But what he enjoyed most was building a narrative for each trade: *what has to happen for this to work?* Then he attacked his own story: *what would make me take the loss, or cut the profit short?* The storyteller builds the narrative; the methodologist tries to break it.

### Chapter 5 — The Call to Adventure
In his first year of psychology, Nika met the thing that would pull every earlier thread together: AI. Fascination became daily use, and daily use became craft. He wrote prompts for specific kinds of academic work and shared them with his cohort, along with AI-generated summaries his classmates came to rely on. Without calling it that, he was helping people by building tools that made their work easier. He completed Anthropic's *Claude 101* and *AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations*. Where others saw a chatbot, he saw workflows.

### Chapter 6 — Crossing the Threshold
After graduating in 2026, Nika faced the expected next step, a master's, and postponed it. He sized the decision like a position: building AI workflows for companies was a field too new for formal curricula, where early movers could gain far more than they risked. So he chose to learn by building. Searching for a method, he found Jake Van Clief's ICM (Interpretable Context Methodology) and joined its community, and later its VIP tier. It matched a conviction from psychological methods: Occam's razor, the simplest structure that does the job.

### Chapter 7 — Allies
A conversation about AI after a yoga class led Nika to a co-founder whose background in consulting, strategy and software engineering covered what Nika lacked. He saw someone with an unusual way of thinking who acted on his ideas instead of just discussing them. Together they began building Milias, an AI workflow consultancy. Nika designs the workflows and the division of work between humans and AI. The first engagement came from close to home: a pro bono pilot for a fruit and vegetable export company.

### Chapter 8 — The Ordeal
At twenty, Nika walked away from film when he saw the struggle it demanded. At twenty-four, he faces the same kind of test and makes the opposite choice. His ordeal is a daily one: postponing reward while peers take the paid and predictable path, and waking up knowing he has to walk toward what makes him uncomfortable. It takes two concrete forms. The first is putting himself in public, with a stint of posting content on TikTok and Instagram. The second is the voice asking whether he's qualified to advise companies at all. He answers it the way he answers every hypothesis: with evidence, delivering real work for free until the results speak for him.

### Chapter 9 — The Reward
The pilot paid nothing, but it gave him rules he could not have learned any other way:
- Working with AI in Europe means answering to both AI law and sector-specific regulation.
- Security is his responsibility and no one else's.
- The most elegant AI workflow can still be the wrong one: use traditional code first, and AI only where nothing simpler will do.
- An automation that costs nearly as much as the process it replaces isn't worth building.

Even stepping back from social media was a lesson: first prove himself to himself and to others, then tell the world.

### Chapter 10 — Return with the Elixir
Nika's elixir is not AI itself; it is *judgment about* AI. Where the market sells hype, he offers realism about what is achievable for this particular company and what isn't. He helps small and mid-sized businesses avoid investments driven by fear of missing out. He puts AI where it genuinely saves costs or raises profits, so they can grow their output without growing their headcount at the same rate. He believes these businesses create much of the world's real value, and that helping them adapt is how he contributes in the long run. The boy who once chased a doctor's title found his own way to help people.
